From lumbar MRI to an accurate radiologist report: where the errors are

Each step shows the current model and its measured result, the best achievable value (ceiling) and how much of the final error this step contributes. The test set is the internal RSNA 2024 split: 308 studies and 1,540 central-canal levels, with the severe threshold frozen on validation at a 5% false-positive cap. Unmeasured values are marked as such and never shown as zero.

Error budget on the 308-study test set

Counts are measured for the sagittal-crop central-canal model. The weights are our hypothesis, not taken from a guideline. A missed severe level or a finding at the wrong level can send the report, and a procedure, to the wrong target. A false alert costs reading time. Set the weights with radiologists; the bars recompute. Hatched rows have no measurement and are left out of the total. The share moved to step 1 is the fraction of that error that disappeared when the same model was retrained and tested on RSNA-landmark crops, averaged over 3 seeds; for missed severe levels the oracle arm also raised false alerts, so its 26% is an upper estimate and not statistically significant.

What this page does not show

Agreement with RSNA labels is not clinical correctness, and a report that follows the grading rule is not proven benefit to the patient. RSNA contains no symptoms, neurological examination, treatment or outcome, so symptom match (step 3), cause (step 4) and outcome-based neighbours (step 6) cannot be measured on it. Urgent findings such as cauda equina compression, tumour, infection or fracture are outside the RSNA labels; the current models do not look for them. The test split was inspected during earlier work, and every number is an internal estimate without site-held-out validation.
